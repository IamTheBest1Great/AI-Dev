## 1. Instruction-Response Datasets

**Q1. [Conceptual] Explain Instruction-Response Datasets in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** The purpose is not merely to expose the model to additional information. It is also to demonstrate the desired relationship between a user's request and the model's response.


**Q2. [Interview] Why does Instruction-Response Datasets matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** The instruction specifies what the model should do, while the response demonstrates the expected output.


<a id="2-demonstrations"></a>
## 2. Demonstrations

**Q3. [Conceptual] Explain Demonstrations in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** A demonstration is an example that shows how a task should be performed.


**Q4. [Interview] Why does Demonstrations matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** Demonstrations make the desired behavior concrete. They show rather than merely describe what a good response should look like.

<a id="3-supervised-fine-tuning-sft"></a>
## 3. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

**Q9. [Conceptual] Explain Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is a training process that adapts a pretrained model using examples of desired inputs and outputs.


**Q10. [Interview] Why does Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** SFT uses supervised examples to shape a pretrained model into a model that is more useful for instruction-following tasks.

## 5. Instruction-Following Evaluation

**Q17. [Conceptual] Explain Instruction-Following Evaluation in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** Answer accuracy: Is the information correct?


**Q18. [Interview] Why does Instruction-Following Evaluation matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** A model may produce fluent language without satisfying the request. Therefore, evaluation must measure both output quality and actual task completion.

## 6. SFT Dataset Quality and Diversity

**Q23. [Conceptual] Explain SFT Dataset Quality and Diversity in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** The dataset provides limited exposure to different kinds of instructions and output requirements.


**Q24. [Interview] Why does SFT Dataset Quality and Diversity matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** The evaluation set should contain examples that are not simply reused as training targets. This allows the team to test whether the desired behavior generalizes beyond the demonstrations used for training.


<a id="dataset-quality"></a>
### Dataset quality

**Q25. [Conceptual] Explain Dataset quality in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** Clear instructions. Correct target responses. Relevant examples. Consistent formatting. Appropriate difficulty. Sufficient coverage of intended tasks.


**Q26. [Interview] Why does Dataset quality matter, and what is one important design implication?**

## 7. Knowledge vs. Behavior

**Q33. [Conceptual] Explain Knowledge vs. Behavior in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** The distinction between knowledge and behavior is useful for learning, but it is not absolute. SFT can also influence the information a model produces, and pre-training already teaches patterns of text generation and interaction.


**Q34. [Interview] Why does Knowledge vs. Behavior matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** The important point is that SFT explicitly trains against examples of the desired responses.
