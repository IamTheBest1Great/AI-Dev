## 1. The Task and the Required Invariants

**Q1. [Conceptual] Explain The Task and the Required Invariants in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** The correct outcome is one authorized task with verified contents. The client should recover the original operation instead of blindly creating another task.


**Q2. [Interview] Why does The Task and the Required Invariants matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** The action creates a reversible internal task without sending notifications. The test policy treats it as a low-risk write covered by the user's explicit instruction and current permission. A separate approval step is therefore not required in this fixture.

## 3. Distinguish the Action Identifiers

**Q9. [Conceptual] Explain Distinguish the Action Identifiers in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** One user request may generate several attempts, a provider operation, a task resource and a receipt. These identities are related but serve different purposes.

<a id="4-understand-the-unsafe-baseline"></a>
## 4. Understand the Unsafe Baseline

**Q11. [Conceptual] Explain Understand the Unsafe Baseline in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** A timeout at T3 is compatible with several provider states. It is not equivalent to an authoritative rejection before execution.


**Q12. [Interview] Why does Understand the Unsafe Baseline matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** Recovery should use the service's operation status, receipts and effect checks. It should not infer outcome from what happened to the client's waiting task.

## 5. Prepare a Durable Client Intent

**Q13. [Conceptual] Explain Prepare a Durable Client Intent in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** A durable intent is a persisted record of the action the client intends to perform, including the identity required to recover it after a restart.


**Q14. [Interview] Why does Prepare a Durable Client Intent matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** Calling the program's Journal.prepare() creates a new action. It must not be called again just because an old attempt timed out. Recovery uses Journal.load().

**Q51. [Conceptual] Explain Security of the journal and receipts in simple, interview-ready language.**
