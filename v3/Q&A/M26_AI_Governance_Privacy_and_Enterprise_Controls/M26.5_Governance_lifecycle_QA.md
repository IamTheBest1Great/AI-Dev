## M26.5.1 — Ownership and review

**Q1. [Interview] What should an engineer understand about Ownership and review (M26.5.1)?**

**Answer:** periodic retirement review.


**Q2. [Scenario] Why could overlooking Ownership and review create problems in a regulated enterprise AI application?**

**Answer:** In a governed enterprise AI service, this changes the decision criteria or the allowed workflow. Record who owns the rule, when it applies and how to verify compliance.

## Cross-cutting production interview drill

These questions are adapted from the source study guide’s interview section and assess how the individual concepts work together.

**Q3. [Interview] Explain governance lifecycle to an interviewer.**

**Answer:** Interview answer: Governance lifecycle addresses the need to assign accountable owners and enforce risk, privacy and data lifecycle policies. I would begin with a bounded design for a governed enterprise AI service, then specify contracts for Ownership and review and State and evidence. I would measure policy compliance and verified deletion coverage, verify the actual outcome and demonstrate handling of the primary failure: a deleted profile remains in embeddings, backups or cached summaries.


**Q4. [Interview] How would you design Ownership and review from scratch?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would identify the trusted input source, decide which component owns the operation, define typed fields, and specify success and failure states. In this part of a governed enterprise AI service, Ownership and review is an explicit design concern. Define its inputs, owner, observable result and failure handling before implementing it. Its purpose must be linked to the overall goal: assign accountable owners and enforce risk, privacy and data lifecycle policies.


**Q5. [Interview] How do State and evidence and Validation interact?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: First define their separate responsibilities. Identify who owns this information, where it lives, and when it changes. Keep transient model context distinct from durable application state and enforce scope on every read and write. Checking input and output data against schemas, required fields and domain constraints before further action. I would connect them only through a typed handoff with provenance and an explicit postcondition, rather than allowing hidden state coupling.


**Q6. [Interview] What would you do if Failure recovery fails in production?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would contain the impact, check trace IDs, state and external receipts, then classify the failure as transient, permanent or unknown outcome. I would not blindly repeat a write. After fixing the cause, I would add the incident as a versioned regression case and measure policy compliance and verified deletion coverage.


**Q7. [Interview] When would you avoid a framework or model for governance lifecycle?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: When the rules and data contracts are deterministic, a small application service or workflow is usually easier to test and operate. I add an agent or new framework only when variable inputs and evidence show a measurable improvement that offsets extra coordination, cost and risk.


**Q8. [Interview] How would you demonstrate L3 production readiness for M26.5?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would present a working repository, a state and permission diagram, the risk register, data inventory and deletion verification record, known limitations, representative evaluation fixtures, failure-injection results and tracing. I would also show restart behavior and an operator takeover path where needed.
