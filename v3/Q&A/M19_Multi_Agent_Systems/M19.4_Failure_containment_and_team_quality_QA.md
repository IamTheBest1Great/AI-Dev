## Cross-cutting production interview drill

These questions are adapted from the source study guide’s interview section and assess how the individual concepts work together.

**Q33. [Interview] Explain failure containment and team quality to an interviewer.**

**Answer:** Interview answer: Failure containment and team quality addresses the need to compare multi-agent coordination against a strong single-workflow baseline. I would begin with a bounded design for a research organization with specialist agents, then specify contracts for Duplication and Conflicting actions. I would measure task quality per cost and coordination-failure rate, verify the actual outcome and demonstrate handling of the primary failure:.


**Q34. [Interview] How would you design Duplication from scratch?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would identify the trusted input source, decide which component owns the operation, define typed fields, and specify success and failure states. Two paths repeat the same computation or external action; prevent with task ownership, deduplication and idempotency. Then I would create at least a happy-path and an unauthorized/invalid-input test.


**Q35. [Interview] How do Conflicting actions and Cascading errors interact?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: First define their separate responsibilities. In this part of a research organization with specialist agents, Conflicting actions is an explicit design concern. Define its inputs, owner, observable result and failure handling before implementing it. Its purpose must be linked to the overall goal: compare multi-agent coordination against a strong single-workflow baseline.


**Q36. [Interview] What would you do if Coordination deadlocks fails in production?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would contain the impact, check trace IDs, state and external receipts, then classify the failure as transient, permanent or unknown outcome. I would not blindly repeat a write. After fixing the cause, I would add the incident as a versioned regression case and measure task quality per cost and coordination-failure rate.


**Q37. [Interview] When would you avoid a framework or model for failure containment and team quality?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: When the rules and data contracts are deterministic, a small application service or workflow is usually easier to test and operate. I add an agent or new framework only when variable inputs and evidence show a measurable improvement that offsets extra coordination, cost and risk.


**Q38. [Interview] How would you demonstrate L3 production readiness for M19.4?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would present a working repository, a state and permission diagram, the single-agent versus multi-agent benchmark and coordination trace, known limitations, representative evaluation fixtures, failure-injection results and tracing. I would also show restart behavior and an operator takeover path where needed.
