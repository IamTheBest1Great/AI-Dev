## M41.2.1 — AI ROI

<a id="hours-saved"></a>
#### Hours saved

**Q1. [Conceptual] What is hours saved, and why does it matter in AI ROI?**

**Answer:** Verified reduction in work time per accepted task multiplied by real task volume and adoption.


**Q2. [Interview] Where would you apply hours saved when designing a customer-facing AI product, and how would you explain your decision?**

**Answer:** Use hours saved in the interview users and map work stage. Define the relevant input, output, or decision; specify a measurable acceptance check instead of trusting a generated description alone.


**Q3. [Scenario] Your customer-facing AI product encounters this problem: a polished chatbot is launched but users have no reason to adopt it. How would you handle it specifically for hours saved?**

**Answer:** To address it, prepare or implement problem-first discovery and observed workflow experiments. Verified reduction in work time per accepted task multiplied by real task volume and adoption. Confirm the change with a reproducible negative test and an observed result.


**Q4. [Project / Real-world] What would you build or demonstrate in a real customer-facing AI product to prove hours saved works?**

**Answer:** During the interview users and map work phase, apply this principle: verified reduction in work time per accepted task multiplied by real task volume and adoption. For evidence, record the input and rule version, then verify the observed result for hours saved. Where applicable, add a negative test that proves the boundary still holds under retries, stale data or unauthorized access.

## Cross-cutting production interview drill

These questions are adapted from the source study guide’s interview section and assess how the individual concepts work together.

**Q29. [Interview] Explain outcome measurement to an interviewer.**

**Answer:** Interview answer: Outcome measurement addresses the need to solve an expensive user workflow and quantify adoption and ROI. I would begin with a bounded design for a commercial AI workflow assistant, then specify contracts for Hours saved and Cost avoided. I would measure time saved, accepted outcomes and retention, verify the actual outcome and demonstrate handling of the primary failure: a polished chatbot is launched but users have no reason to adopt it.


**Q30. [Interview] How would you design Hours saved from scratch?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would identify the trusted input source, decide which component owns the operation, define typed fields, and specify success and failure states. Verified reduction in work time per accepted task multiplied by real task volume and adoption. Then I would create at least a happy-path and an unauthorized/invalid-input test.


**Q31. [Interview] How do Cost avoided and Throughput increase interact?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: First define their separate responsibilities. Measured expense prevented by better workflow outcomes, net of new AI and review costs. Specify the measured unit, attribution owner and ceiling. Include failed attempts, shared resources and burst behavior so a local optimization does not hide system-level expense. I would connect them only through a typed handoff with provenance and an explicit postcondition, rather than allowing hidden state coupling.


**Q32. [Interview] What would you do if Error reduction fails in production?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would contain the impact, check trace IDs, state and external receipts, then classify the failure as transient, permanent or unknown outcome. I would not blindly repeat a write. After fixing the cause, I would add the incident as a versioned regression case and measure time saved, accepted outcomes and retention.


**Q33. [Interview] When would you avoid a framework or model for outcome measurement?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: When the rules and data contracts are deterministic, a small application service or workflow is usually easier to test and operate. I add an agent or new framework only when variable inputs and evidence show a measurable improvement that offsets extra coordination, cost and risk.


**Q34. [Interview] How would you demonstrate L3 production readiness for M41.2?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would present a working repository, a state and permission diagram, the problem brief, workflow ROI model and prioritized backlog, known limitations, representative evaluation fixtures, failure-injection results and tracing. I would also show restart behavior and an operator takeover path where needed.
