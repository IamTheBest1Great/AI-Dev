## Cross-cutting production interview drill

These questions are adapted from the source study guide’s interview section and assess how the individual concepts work together.

**Q53. [Interview] Explain state, waiting and recovery to an interviewer.**

**Answer:** Interview answer: State, waiting and recovery addresses the need to resume after crashes without duplicating real-world side effects. I would begin with a bounded design for a multi-hour evidence collection and report workflow, then specify contracts for Durable workflows and Checkpointing. I would measure exactly-one business outcome under replay tests, verify the actual outcome and demonstrate handling of the primary failure:.


**Q54. [Interview] How would you design Durable workflows from scratch?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would identify the trusted input source, decide which component owns the operation, define typed fields, and specify success and failure states. Workflows whose task state and transition history survive worker restarts and extended waits. Then I would create at least a happy-path and an unauthorized/invalid-input test.


**Q55. [Interview] How do Checkpointing and Workflow state interact?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: First define their separate responsibilities. Persist enough state to resume a computation from a known boundary. It protects workflow progress but does not itself make remote side effects atomic. Typed status and data needed to decide the next action after a crash or a signal. I would connect them only through a typed handoff with provenance and an explicit postcondition, rather than allowing hidden state coupling.


**Q56. [Interview] What would you do if Event sourcing concepts fails in production?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would contain the impact, check trace IDs, state and external receipts, then classify the failure as transient, permanent or unknown outcome. I would not blindly repeat a write. After fixing the cause, I would add the incident as a versioned regression case and measure exactly-one business outcome under replay tests.


**Q57. [Interview] When would you avoid a framework or model for state, waiting and recovery?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: When the rules and data contracts are deterministic, a small application service or workflow is usually easier to test and operate. I add an agent or new framework only when variable inputs and evidence show a measurable improvement that offsets extra coordination, cost and risk.


**Q58. [Interview] How would you demonstrate L3 production readiness for M14.2?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would present a working repository, a state and permission diagram, the durable execution history and idempotency receipts, known limitations, representative evaluation fixtures, failure-injection results and tracing. I would also show restart behavior and an operator takeover path where needed.
