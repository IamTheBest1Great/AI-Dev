## Cross-cutting production interview drill

These questions are adapted from the source study guide’s interview section and assess how the individual concepts work together.

**Q45. [Interview] Explain job lifecycle and correctness to an interviewer.**

**Answer:** Interview answer: Job lifecycle and correctness addresses the need to run fair, observable work with restart and cancellation semantics. I would begin with a bounded design for a background job system for scheduled AI tasks, then specify contracts for Queues and Scheduled jobs. I would measure completion latency, retry rate and duplicate side effects, verify the actual outcome and demonstrate handling of the primary failure:.


**Q46. [Interview] How would you design Queues from scratch?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would identify the trusted input source, decide which component owns the operation, define typed fields, and specify success and failure states. Durable message buffers connecting producers to workers; delivery is commonly at-least-once and consumers require idempotency. Then I would create at least a happy-path and an unauthorized/invalid-input test.


**Q47. [Interview] How do Scheduled jobs and Event-driven jobs interact?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: First define their separate responsibilities. Tasks initiated by calendar or time rules; store execution IDs and compensate for missed or duplicated schedule triggers. Workloads started by a verified external event rather than by an interactive request. I would connect them only through a typed handoff with provenance and an explicit postcondition, rather than allowing hidden state coupling.


**Q48. [Interview] What would you do if Dead-letter queues fails in production?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would contain the impact, check trace IDs, state and external receipts, then classify the failure as transient, permanent or unknown outcome. I would not blindly repeat a write. After fixing the cause, I would add the incident as a versioned regression case and measure completion latency, retry rate and duplicate side effects.


**Q49. [Interview] When would you avoid a framework or model for job lifecycle and correctness?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: When the rules and data contracts are deterministic, a small application service or workflow is usually easier to test and operate. I add an agent or new framework only when variable inputs and evidence show a measurable improvement that offsets extra coordination, cost and risk.


**Q50. [Interview] How would you demonstrate L3 production readiness for M37.1?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would present a working repository, a state and permission diagram, the job state ledger and worker operations dashboard, known limitations, representative evaluation fixtures, failure-injection results and tracing. I would also show restart behavior and an operator takeover path where needed.
