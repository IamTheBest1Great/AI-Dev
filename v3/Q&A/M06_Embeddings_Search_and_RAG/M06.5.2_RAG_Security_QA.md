## 1. Security Fundamentals and Trust Boundaries

**Q1. [Conceptual] Explain Security Fundamentals and Trust Boundaries in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** The application has more sensitive surfaces than the answer text. A filename can reveal a confidential project. A similarity score can reveal that a hidden document probably exists. A cached summary can preserve a deleted source. An embedding is derived from content and should not automatically be treated as anonymous data.


**Q2. [Interview] Why does Security Fundamentals and Trust Boundaries matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** These permissions are not interchangeable. A user may read a confidential source while organizational policy prohibits sending it to an external model. A model that can answer questions should not automatically obtain write credentials.

## 2. M06.5.2.1 — Tenant Isolation

**Q3. [Conceptual] Explain M06.5.2.1 — Tenant Isolation in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** A tenant is an administrative or customer boundary within a shared application. Tenant isolation prevents one tenant's requests from reading, modifying, or learning protected information belonging to another tenant.


**Q4. [Interview] Why does M06.5.2.1 — Tenant Isolation matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** A tenant may contain additional boundaries. Tenant A can own Ship A and Ship C, while an individual user is assigned only to Ship A. A tenant filter alone therefore may be necessary but insufficient.

## 4. M06.5.2.1 — Retrieval-Time Authorization

**Q11. [Conceptual] Explain M06.5.2.1 — Retrieval-Time Authorization in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** Retrieval-time authorization determines which evidence the current requester is allowed to use when searching and assembling an answer.


**Q12. [Interview] Why does M06.5.2.1 — Retrieval-Time Authorization matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** Authorization and retrieval quality are separate requirements. Searching globally and then filtering a small top-K can fail to recover relevant permitted documents outside that global top-K. Increasing K can reduce the problem but does not create a general completeness guarantee.

**Q39. [Conceptual] Explain M06.5.2.2 — Prompt Injection and Malicious Documents in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** An indirect injection arrives through retrieved material, such as a document, webpage, image, OCR layer, or tool result. The attacker is attempting to promote source content into authority over the application.

## 7. M06.5.2.2 — Poisoning and Information Leakage

**Q45. [Conceptual] Explain M06.5.2.2 — Poisoning and Information Leakage in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** Record who provided a source, its authoritative location, revision, approval state, and a content hash. A hash detects differences from an expected value; it does not prove the content is true or harmless.


**Q46. [Interview] Why does M06.5.2.2 — Poisoning and Information Leakage matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** If an attacker can change both a document and its stored hash, a matching hash is not useful tamper evidence. Protect the manifest separately where integrity assurance is required. Content approval and access approval remain separate decisions.

**Q47. [Conceptual] Explain M06.5.2.3 — Auditability in simple, interview-ready language.**

## 9. Revocation, Deletion, and In-Flight Requests

**Q53. [Conceptual] Explain Revocation, Deletion, and In-Flight Requests in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** When a source is revoked or deleted, first make it ineligible at the authoritative serving gate. Then remove or invalidate derived records according to the deletion workflow.


**Q54. [Interview] Why does Revocation, Deletion, and In-Flight Requests matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** The gate should block stale vector hits, sparse hits, summaries, cached responses, parent expansions, and citation previews. Physical deletion can take longer because stores may compact data asynchronously. Backups and audit retention need explicit policies; do not claim immediate physical erasure unless it was actually verified.
