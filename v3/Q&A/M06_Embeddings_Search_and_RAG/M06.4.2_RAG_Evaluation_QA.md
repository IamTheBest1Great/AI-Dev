## 1. Evaluation Fundamentals

**Q1. [Conceptual] Explain Evaluation Fundamentals in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** Evaluate the pipeline at several stages so improvements are attributable and failures are diagnosable.


**Q2. [Interview] Why does Evaluation Fundamentals matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** The RAGAs paper and ARES paper evaluate distinct aspects of RAG rather than treating answer quality as one undifferentiated property. Their methods are useful references, but metric names and implementations can vary by framework and version.

### Evaluation as a controlled comparison

**Q3. [Conceptual] Explain Evaluation as a controlled comparison in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** Compare systems on the same questions, source snapshot, authorization states, labels, and metric definitions. Record which component changed.

## 3. M06.4.2.1 — Recall@K and Precision@K

**Q13. [Conceptual] Explain M06.4.2.1 — Recall@K and Precision@K in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** If three evidence items are relevant and two appear in the top five, recall@5 is \(2/3\approx0.667\).


**Q14. [Interview] Why does M06.4.2.1 — Recall@K and Precision@K matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** High recall with low precision can give the reranker a large useful pool mixed with noise. High precision with low recall can omit a necessary exception or second source.

<a id="5-ndcg"></a>
## 5. nDCG

**Q17. [Conceptual] Explain nDCG in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** It is helpful when evidence has different degrees of usefulness. A passage directly answering the central question should often rank above a vaguely related background passage.


**Q18. [Interview] Why does nDCG matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain, or nDCG, evaluates a ranked list with graded relevance and discounts useful items that appear farther down the ranking.


<a id="why-normalization-matters"></a>
### Why normalization matters

**Q19. [Conceptual] Explain Why normalization matters in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** Raw DCG is larger for queries with more highly relevant items. Dividing by the query’s ideal ranking makes the score comparable relative to that query’s available relevance.


**Q20. [Interview] Why does Why normalization matters matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** For nonnegative grades and a correctly constructed ideal ranking, nDCG lies between 0 and 1.

## 12. Human Evaluation

**Q51. [Conceptual] Explain Human Evaluation in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** Human reviewers can examine source meaning, domain-specific conditions, and subtle failures that automated checks miss. They are also fallible and need consistent rubrics.

<a id="14-online-evaluation-and-a-b-tests"></a>
## 14. Online Evaluation and A/B Tests

**Q63. [Conceptual] Explain Online Evaluation and A/B Tests in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** Offline tests are better for diagnosis and release gates. Online tests reveal actual task patterns, latency under load, and user behavior that curated datasets may miss.


**Q64. [Interview] Why does Online Evaluation and A/B Tests matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** Clicks, thumbs-up, and answer length are imperfect proxies. A user may like a confident but wrong answer, or fail to click a citation because the answer was sufficient.


<a id="15-permissions-deletion-and-operational-gates"></a>
## 15. Permissions, Deletion, and Operational Gates

**Q65. [Conceptual] Explain Permissions, Deletion, and Operational Gates in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** A final answer that happens not to reveal a forbidden chunk is insufficient if that chunk was already sent to an external model. Inspect the entire evidence path.


**Q66. [Interview] Why does Permissions, Deletion, and Operational Gates matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** Logical non-serving and physical byte removal are different guarantees. Test and report the relevant policy instead of using the word “deleted” ambiguously.
