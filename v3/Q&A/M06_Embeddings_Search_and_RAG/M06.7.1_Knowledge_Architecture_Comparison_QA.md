## 3. Fine-Tuning

**Q5. [Conceptual] Explain Fine-Tuning in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** LoRA is a primary example of parameter-efficient adaptation using trainable low-rank components while keeping original model weights fixed during that adaptation. Its suitability and actual resource savings depend on the model and implementation. Source: Hu et al., LoRA.

**Answer:** Fine-tuning is a behavior intervention in this example. The current procedure should still come from an authoritative source.

### Security and deletion still matter

**Q17. [Conceptual] Explain Security and deletion still matter in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** Cached prompts, conversation histories and saved summaries are derived copies. A source deletion policy must account for those copies. A new document set should not silently reuse cached material from a previous permission state.


**Q18. [Interview] Why does Security and deletion still matter matter, and what is one important design implication?**

## 5. Knowledge Graphs

**Q21. [Conceptual] Explain Knowledge Graphs in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** A knowledge graph represents entities and relationships. An asset can be connected to its component, a component to its supplier, and a supplier to a service bulletin.


**Q22. [Interview] Why does Knowledge Graphs matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** Graph retrieval is useful when the question is naturally relational: “Which assets are affected by parts from supplier S?” or “How is this component linked to this bulletin?”.

## 6. SQL and Structured Retrieval

**Q27. [Conceptual] Explain SQL and Structured Retrieval in simple, interview-ready language.**

**Answer:** Structured retrieval queries information whose fields and relationships are explicitly represented. SQL commonly supports filtering, joins, sorting and aggregation over relational tables.


**Q28. [Interview] Why does SQL and Structured Retrieval matter, and what is one important design implication?**

**Answer:** Use database roles, constrained views, application authorization and row-level policies where appropriate. PostgreSQL documents both the behavior of row-security policies and important bypass conditions for owners or privileged roles. Test the actual application role rather than assuming the existence of a policy is sufficient. Source: PostgreSQL row security.
