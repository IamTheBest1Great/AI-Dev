#### SSO

**Q1. [Conceptual] What is sSO, and why does it matter in Enterprise?**

**Answer:** In this part of a subscription-based multi-company AI SaaS, SSO is an explicit design concern. Define its inputs, owner, observable result and failure handling before implementing it. Its purpose must be linked to the overall goal: enforce tenant isolation, plan limits and correct metering.


**Q2. [Interview] Where would you apply sSO when designing a multi-tenant SaaS application, and how would you explain your decision?**

## Cross-cutting production interview drill

These questions are adapted from the source study guide’s interview section and assess how the individual concepts work together.

**Q29. [Interview] Explain enterprise administration to an interviewer.**

**Answer:** Interview answer: Enterprise administration addresses the need to enforce tenant isolation, plan limits and correct metering. I would begin with a bounded design for a subscription-based multi-company AI SaaS, then specify contracts for SSO and Audit logs. I would measure cross-tenant access violations and billing reconciliation correctness, verify the actual outcome and demonstrate handling of the primary failure: company A can retrieve company B’s training material through a cached answer.


**Q30. [Interview] How would you design SSO from scratch?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would identify the trusted input source, decide which component owns the operation, define typed fields, and specify success and failure states. In this part of a subscription-based multi-company AI SaaS, SSO is an explicit design concern. Define its inputs, owner, observable result and failure handling before implementing it. Its purpose must be linked to the overall goal: enforce tenant isolation, plan limits and correct metering.


**Q31. [Interview] How do Audit logs and Admin controls interact?**

**Answer:** Follow-up 1 — What would you test first? A negative case involving data residency and a normal task with a verified outcome.


**Q32. [Interview] What would you do if Data retention fails in production?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would contain the impact, check trace IDs, state and external receipts, then classify the failure as transient, permanent or unknown outcome. I would not blindly repeat a write. After fixing the cause, I would add the incident as a versioned regression case and measure cross-tenant access violations and billing reconciliation correctness.


**Q33. [Interview] When would you avoid a framework or model for enterprise administration?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: When the rules and data contracts are deterministic, a small application service or workflow is usually easier to test and operate. I add an agent or new framework only when variable inputs and evidence show a measurable improvement that offsets extra coordination, cost and risk.


**Q34. [Interview] How would you demonstrate L3 production readiness for M42.4?**

**Answer:** Interview answer: I would present a working repository, a state and permission diagram, the tenant-isolation test suite and usage/billing ledger, known limitations, representative evaluation fixtures, failure-injection results and tracing. I would also show restart behavior and an operator takeover path where needed.
